# Found details

Twelve Commons photos (licences and authors in `credits.json`), searched in English, German
and French on 2026-10-08. Each line is something I saw in a photo, with where.

## The convention that matters most

1. **The high-water plaque.** Three countries, one grammar: a horizontal line across the
   plate, the event above it, the date below it, fixed at the exact height the water reached.
   Buffumville: "High Water Mark / 32.5 feet / April 1987", white plate, a thin red line, two
   black-headed screws (flood-gauge-staff-2). King's Lynn: brushed steel, engraved serif,
   "High tide level / 5th December 2013", four domed rivets, a hairline crack running down
   the plate (high-water-mark-plaque-0, -1). Moscow: cast iron, raised condensed capitals,
   "Water level / 11 April 1908", two cross-head screws, set into flaking oxide-red paint
   (high-water-mark-plaque-2). A Watermark evidence pack is this plaque for one home: what
   happened, the line, the date.
2. **Béhuard's flood board** (chelle-de-crue-1): a slate board on a limestone wall with an
   E-pattern staff, and for each historic flood a white diamond at its height, a dark tag
   "6,78 m (1910)" pointing at it, and a dated photograph beside it. This is an incident
   history drawn as an object: the dashboard.

## Marks and lettering

3. E-shaped divisions alternating left and right on the staff, navy on white (chelle-de-crue-1)
   and black on white (pegel-hochwasser-2, small staff by the steps).
4. Checkered scale: a column of small black and white squares beside stencil-heavy
   numerals 25 to 40, numerals overlapping the check column (flood-gauge-staff-2).
5. Pennywort Creek: "m" above a big "2", decimals 1.8 to 0.2 in plain grotesk, short black
   tick dashes on both edges, big numerals only at whole metres (flood-gauge-staff-1).
6. Hauxton staff: tiny numerals repeating 1 to 9 with a red or bold 10 at each metre
   (flood-gauge-staff-0).
7. Avignon: the staff laid on a sloped concrete ramp, its red numbered strip running
   diagonally down into the river (chelle-de-crue-0). A gauge does not have to be vertical.
8. 1920s staff: Roman numerals I to XI with slanted dash ticks on a weathered post, a man
   pointing up at where the water used to be (chelle-de-crue-2).

## Materials and setting

9. Concrete with stain streaks running down from the top, darker where water stood
   (flood-gauge-staff-2, chelle-de-crue-0 bridge piers).
10. Limestone blocks, warm grey-beige, pitted (chelle-de-crue-1): #D3C2AE, #BCAA90.
11. Slate board, warm near-black: #29211B, #423528 (chelle-de-crue-1).
12. Brushed steel plate: #C0C2C4 (high-water-mark-plaque-0).
13. Cast iron: #2F1612; oxide-red painted wall #D3704F (high-water-mark-plaque-2).
14. The plaque's red line, sampled from its core pixels: #740C13 (crop of flood-gauge-staff-2).
15. Domed rivets and screw heads at the plate corners, always two or four, always symmetric.
16. Cracked dry mud around the old staff in a drought (chelle-de-crue-2): what "no water" looks like.

## My own floods (demo/broll/flood, not committed)

17. Drip trails: thin, wavering vertical lines running from the basement window sill down
    beige drywall to the baseboard, each one a slightly different path (20260620_184158.jpg).
    The most literal watermark in the set, and it is on my wall.
18. Water on pale oak flooring reads as glossy darker patches with bright specular dots,
    not as a colour (20260620_163820.mp4, 172952.jpg). Water is a darkening, never a hue.
19. The window well under the deck: a corrugated steel half-pipe full of brown water,
    against brick (20260620_182340.mp4).
20. December: an opened ceiling with wet joists and pipes, a replacement fill valve in its
    blister pack (20251205_114716.jpg, 120430.jpg).
21. First anchor test: a red puddle reads as blood. Red stays a line or a mark.

## What this changes

- The logo is a high-water plaque: a riveted plate split by the line.
- The signature device is the high-water line: one red rule with a rivet at each end, the
  event above it and the date below it, at the level on every screen that records water.
- Incident history becomes a Béhuard board: marks at their heights, tags pointing at them.
- The wet accent moves from an invented pink to the plaque's red line.
